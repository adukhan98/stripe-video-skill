# stripe-youtube — planning: chapters, beats, cadence

A reel is ~15 designed scenes back to back. A YouTube video is 8–15 minutes — designing every beat would be tiring to
watch and impossible to build. Both references (Eric Tech, Nick Saraev) and Greg Isenberg keep a board on screen most
of the time with the speaker on a small card, and use full-frame talk as punctuation. So plan in two levels.

## 1. Chapter outline (approval #1)

From the corrected transcript, propose 4–9 chapters (a numbered framework — "11 habits" — can be one chapter per item;
YouTube chapters ≥ 10 s each). For each: start time (on a sentence boundary), title (≤ 5 words, his wording), the icon
for its disc (Phosphor name), and the one designed moment that carries it. Also: the look (v7 MINT by default — no
need to ask), the map (the framework every chapter hangs on, as a disc chain — if the video has one), the hook (first
30 s: what he promises, which moment proves it), the CTA, the music plan. Show it as a table and wait.

| # | Start | Chapter | Disc icon | Carrying moment | Real things named |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Under it: running time, Cap trims kept / extra cuts made, theme, reel-able moments (2–3 self-contained 30–60 s stretches
worth cutting into reels with /adnaan-reel), open questions.

## 2. Beat table per chapter (approval #2)

Inside each chapter, split into windows on sentence boundaries and pick a layout for each (references/style.md):

| # | Out time | He says | Layout | What happens | Real assets | SFX |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules of thumb (content-dependent notes in the table, not hard gates):

- **Base mix**: board layouts (canvas / map / board / proof / screen) ~70–85 % of the runtime, talk ~15–30 %.
  Intro, opinions, stories, transitions and the CTA are talk; anything he explains, shows or counts is on the board.
- **Something changes at least every 20–40 s**: a new layout, a new object, a zoom, a callout. A static stretch > 40 s
  gets a reason in the table.
- **A designed moment every ~30–60 s** (canvas, map, board, proof, callout). Scenes are 3–8 s (a recap or proof up to
  ~10 s); a stretch longer than ~8 s is a talk or screen window, not one long scene. IMG_2533 (6 min, advice talk) ran
  43 opaque scenes ≈ 70 % board, and he liked the density.
- **Every chapter starts with its map push or chapter heading** (a chain push to the current disc, or a board/canvas
  with the numbered heading), and the map scene doubles as a YouTube chapter marker (layout.json "chapters").
- **Talk captions: every full-frame talk window gets full-coverage kinetic captions** (he asked for this, 2026-10-17) —
  generated by `talkcaps.py` after the scene list is final; not planned per line. The SRT is still delivered.
- **Screen**: whenever a screen recording shows the thing; zooms on what he clicks (Cap zooms > clicks > manual
  targets in layout.json); a callout once the zoom has landed on the thing he names. Waiting (loading, an AI thinking)
  is cut out, never sped up.
- **Jump cuts** are hidden by the board/card; in long talk stretches cover a jump cut with a layout change or a scene
  rather than a punch-in. Look-downs left at line starts are frozen by `lookdowns.py`, not covered.
- **Hook (0:00–0:30)**: a talk open (captioned) on the promise, then a proof/canvas moment by ~0:15 that shows the
  payoff is real; a short teaser of the map is good. No logo intro, no "hey guys".
- **End**: a recap that walks the map, then the CTA in talk; the last 20 s keep important things out of the end-screen area.

## 3. Files the plan becomes

- `layout.json`: theme, card face position, talk/screen windows (OUTPUT seconds) with per-window zoom targets,
  chapters, music (+ `music_under_voice_db`, default 5), captions (`y`, `x`, `heroes`, `fix` — see talkcaps.py).
- `animations/scenes.json`: `"look": "v7"`, every designed moment and callout — id, type, start (output s), duration,
  assets, brief (exact words + times, the hero object + micro-animations, the clean-stroke work, the structural SFX if
  any). Write the briefs in v7 terms (discs, rings, bars, flat cards) — briefs written for the marker look had to be
  translated by every agent. Shared objects used by several scenes (the chain) get ONE owner and a shared module +
  notes file (`examples/yt-01-v7/CHAIN_SHARED.md`). Talk caption overlays are added by talkcaps.py, not here.
- `compose.py plan` prints the resulting window table — compare it with the beat table before building.
